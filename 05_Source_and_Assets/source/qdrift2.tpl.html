  • Drift: natural mode changes plus injected abrupt, gradual and recurring drift.
  • Baselines: fixed quantum SVM, statically optimised quantum SVM, classical RBF one-class SVM, online classical model, NASA’s LSTM.
  • Metrics: event-level F1, PR-AUC, false alarms per day, detection delay, recovery time, circuit evaluations per sample.

Abstract

Q-DRIFT: Drift-Aware Quantum Kernel Adaptation for Spacecraft Telemetry Anomaly Detection. Venkatanathan O R V (RA2411026011163), Mayukh Sarkar (RA2411026011218), Aman Salarpuria (RA2411003012435).

Spacecraft send a continuous stream of sensor readings, called telemetry, back to Earth. Mission teams rely on anomaly-detection models to flag faults early, but these models are trained on past behaviour. When a spacecraft changes operating mode, or a sensor slowly degrades, the data distribution shifts. This is known as concept drift. A model trained on the old distribution then raises false alarms and can miss real faults.

Quantum kernel methods are a promising family of quantum machine learning models for anomaly detection. A quantum feature map encodes each reading into a quantum state, and the kernel measures similarity between states. Their performance depends strongly on this encoding circuit, yet existing work chooses the encoding once, offline, and never revisits it.

We propose Q-DRIFT, a drift-aware quantum kernel framework for spacecraft telemetry. A quantum-kernel one-class Support Vector Machine produces anomaly scores from windowed telemetry, reduced by PCA to 4–8 values and encoded on 4–8 simulated qubits. An online drift detector (ADWIN) monitors the stream of scores. When drift is detected, a feature-map controller evaluates a small library of candidate encodings (angle, ZZ and IQP feature maps at several bandwidths and depths) on a rolling window using kernel-target alignment. It then switches to the best candidate after a penalty for quantum circuit cost, without retraining the pipeline from scratch. Encodings that worked for a mode are cached and reused when that mode recurs.

We will evaluate Q-DRIFT on the NASA SMAP and Mars Science Laboratory telemetry benchmark (82 channels, 105 expert-labelled anomaly sequences) under natural and injected abrupt, gradual and recurring drift. It will be compared with a fixed quantum SVM, a statically optimised quantum SVM, a classical RBF one-class SVM and an online classical model, using event-level F1, PR-AUC, false alarms, detection delay, recovery time after drift and circuit evaluations per sample. To our knowledge, this is the first drift-adaptive quantum kernel method evaluated on real spacecraft telemetry.

Keywords: quantum machine learning, quantum kernel, quantum support vector machine, concept drift, anomaly detection, spacecraft telemetry, continual learning, NISQ.
